## Methodology note (not a bug, but worth a product decision)

**The pHash algorithm degenerates to an identical hash for flat/solid-color images.** `backend/src/lib/phash.ts` implements an 8×8 difference-hash (compare each pixel to its right-hand neighbor after shrinking to grayscale). My first two synthetic test images were plain solid-color rectangles (different colors, different dimensions) and were incorrectly flagged as duplicates of each other — a Hamming distance of 0, because a genuinely flat image has no left-right pixel differences anywhere in the grid, regardless of what the flat color actually is. I confirmed this was a test-image artifact, not a real dedup bug, by re-running with textured/gradient/noise images: those produced correct true-positive (exact re-upload → duplicate) and true-negative (two different photos → both processed independently) results. Still worth flagging to Developer/Planner: real users do sometimes upload genuinely flat/low-detail photos (blank walls, clear sky, snow, minimalist product shots), and those will collide under this hash the same way my test images did, risking false-positive "duplicate" flags that silently skip classification for a legitimately new photo. Not spec-violating (the spec only asks for "hamming distance < 10", which is implemented correctly) — just a real-world edge case worth a conscious decision (accept the false-positive rate at this stage, or add an exact-file-hash check as a cheap first pass before falling back to pHash for near-duplicates).
